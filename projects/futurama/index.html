Bottom line. Bender’s most-used word is his own name (206 times), then “fry” (158). “Ass” is only his 7th most-used word, but he says it 67 times, more than anyone else in the show.

In “War Is the H-Word,” the plot turns on a bomb that will go off when Bender says his most-used word. Fans have always assumed they know what that word is. I counted every word Bender says across all 140 episodes to check.

His top word is his own name

Bender talks about Bender a lot. After removing common words like “the” and “I,” and filler like “hey” and “yeah,” his own name tops the list, followed by his best friend’s.

rate_data <- totals |>
  filter(words >= 2000) |>
  left_join(select(ass_users, speaker, ass_exact), by = "speaker") |>

How this was done

NoteMethods and limitations

Data. Transcripts of all 140 broadcast episodes from The Infosphere, collected in 2023. The 140 include the four films, which aired as 16 “Part” episodes. Only aggregated counts are published here; the transcripts and scraping code are not.

Cleaning. Most transcript pages had never been split into speaker and line, so I re-split every line on its first colon. That kept 1,010 lines that contain a second colon, which my 2023 version cut off. Stage directions in brackets and parentheses are removed from both the speaker and the line. Alternate-universe, robot, and ghost versions of the main cast count as the same character. The result is 28,949 spoken lines.

Words. Tokenized with tidytext. Common words are removed using tidytext’s stop-word lists, plus interjections and filler (“hey,” “yeah,” “uh,” “gonna,” and stretched spellings like “ohhhh”). Stemming uses the Porter stemmer. Ties share a rank.

Why remove filler. With stop words alone, “hey” edges out “bender” as his top word:

Definition Top five
Stop words and filler removed (used here) bender (206), fry (158), robot (123), time (84), wait (83)
Stop words only hey (212), bender (206), yeah (172), fry (158), robot (123)

What changed from 2023. The original version of this analysis (published on LinkedIn) counted “bender” 375 times. That was inflated: its cleaning removed parenthetical directions but left bracketed ones like “[Bender walks in]” in the dialogue.

Limitations. Two episodes are mostly missing from the source: “Naturama” is one unsplit block of text and “Saturday Morning Fun Pit” largely is too, so those blocks are left out. “Zapp Dingbat” and “Meanwhile” have short transcripts at the source. Fan transcripts also miss or mishear some lines.

## Methods and limitations

**Data.** Transcripts of all 140 broadcast episodes from [The Infosphere](https://theinfosphere.org), collected in 2023. The 140 include the four films, which aired as 16 "Part" episodes. Only aggregated counts are published here; the transcripts and scraping code are not.

**Cleaning.** Most transcript pages had never been split into speaker and line, so I re-split every line on its first colon. The result is `r format(step("Clean spoken lines"), big.mark = ",")` spoken lines.

**Words.** Tokenized with tidytext. Common words are removed using tidytext's stop-word lists, plus interjections and filler ("hey," "yeah," "uh," "gonna," and stretched spellings like "ohhhh"). Stemming uses the Porter stemmer. Ties share a rank.

**Why remove filler.** With stop words alone, "hey" edges out "bender" as his top word:

```{r}
#| label: sensitivity
#| echo: false
sens |>
  filter(rank <= 5) |>
  group_by(definition) |>
  summarise(`Top five` = paste0(word, " (", n, ")", collapse = ", "), .groups = "drop") |>
  mutate(definition = recode(definition,
    "also excluding fillers" = "Stop words and filler removed (used here)",
    "tidytext stop words only" = "Stop words only")) |>
  rename(Definition = definition) |>
  knitr::kable()
```
